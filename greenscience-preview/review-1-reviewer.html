Summer 2027

Enzymatic clean-up to replace HPLC purification of oligos

Alex Morgan · alex.morgan@neb.com · Research · Scientist

Submitted 24 Nov 2026 · last edited 24 Nov 2026

Shortlisted

Proposal

Most modified oligonucleotides we make are purified by reverse-phase HPLC. Each purification uses about 4 L of acetonitrile-containing mobile phase, which leaves the building as hazardous waste, and the step ties up instruments and people for most of a day per batch. We propose an enzymatic clean-up instead. The full-length product carries a protective group at its 5' end; truncated synthesis products do not. An exonuclease such as RecJf digests the unprotected truncations and leaves the protected full-length oligo intact. A short desalting step then removes the enzyme and nucleotides. Early gels (attached) show the truncations disappearing while the full-length band is retained. The project would establish reaction conditions (enzyme amount, time, temperature, buffer) for a panel of 12 oligos of different lengths and modifications, and compare purity, yield, hands-on time and solvent use side by side with our current HPLC method. Expected benefit: about 4 L of solvent avoided per oligo purified. At current volumes that is on the order of 1,500 L of hazardous waste per year, plus instrument time freed for other work. If it works broadly, the method could also be offered to customers as a greener alternative to HPLC.

What the student would do

Run the exonuclease clean-up on the 12-oligo panel, optimise conditions, analyse purity by gel and LC-MS with our help, and work with the Sustainability team to turn the solvent and waste numbers into an impact estimate.
